“I don’t understand what’s happening. Seymour never says anything that would hurt his Sybil. And in his poetry, Shade does nothing but praise her. He is emphatically in love. Gnosticism’s Sybil is in direct opposition to the Demiurge. I’ve done so much for you. That’s what they all have in common.”

He shuffles his feet in the passenger’s seat, not knowing what to say.

“It’s not even true what I said about The Merchant of Venice, by the way. I mean, we did read it, but now that I think about it, we also read Hamlet. I was just—I don’t know. I actually remember it pretty well, truth be told. I would, wouldn’t I? There’s that one scene where he yells at Ophelia to go to a nunnery or something like that? Like you said, we always think we’re so unique, don’t we?”

“And she kills herself pretty shortly after that,” Sybil interjects.

Sheets of rain beat against the windshield.

They’re already back at the apartment, but they’ve been parked outside, sitting in the car talking, unable to move.
