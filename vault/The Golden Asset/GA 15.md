The scenes are structurally repetitive: a suitor arrives, deliberates, picks a casket, opens it, finds what's inside, and either celebrates or leaves. Morocco picks gold (gets a skull), Aragon picks silver (gets a fool's head), Bassanio picks lead (gets the portrait and Portia). Staging this in the discovery space turns the casket scenes into a kind of ritual unveiling. The curtain stays closed while Portia and the suitor talk on the main stage. When the moment of choice comes, the curtain is drawn back to ‘discover’ the three caskets arranged inside the recess — probably on a small table or pedestals, lit somehow to draw the eye. The suitor crosses upstage into the discovery space (or reaches into it) to make his choice. Then the chosen casket is opened, and what's inside is itself a second discovery — the skull, the fool's head, the portrait. Do you get how it works?”
