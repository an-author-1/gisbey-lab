“But isn’t that a particular word for it to choose?”

“What word? Offstage?”

He doesn’t answer her, lost in thought.

“Or did you mean analyst? What word do you mean?”

“Discovery,” he interjects, brightening. “Isn’t that such a funny word?”

“What do you mean, like adventure discovery or the phase where you put out all the evidence and testimony that’s needed for a trial? Either way, what does any of that have to do with anything?”

“No, sorry, I meant the term offstage was odd.”

“Well, discovery is also an offstage space,” she inserts. “It was a staged reveal of sorts. This was back in the Globe Theater days. It was a small curtained recess at the back of the tiring-house wall, set between the two stage doors. Think of it like the red curtain in Twin Peaks. Or the agemaku of Noh Theater. Or also what was behind it. You’d sit there, before the performance, staring at this mask in your trembling hands before you put it on, knowing full well that pretty soon it's going to be glued to your face forever. You’d think that’d get pretty terrifying.”
