He grabs the biggest Band-Aid he can find in a first aid kit in his classroom.

Cleans himself up in the single-use restroom.

Smashes the Band-Aid on his wrist.

As he’s walking down the stairs towards the back entrance of the building—in case the police do come or she’s still at the front gate—the tinkle razor falls out of his pocket.

He grabs it and puts it back.

A locked bathroom in a gas station would be safer.

He has a ways to walk through a neighborhood and isn’t sure which turn to take to get there.

He gets out his phone to check his GPS.

It’s dead.

If he chickens out, there’s no one to call to pick him up.
