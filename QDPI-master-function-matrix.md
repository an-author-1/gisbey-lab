**QDPI master function matrix · Revised 18 September 2026**

This revision incorporates decisions 1–3: creation follows the active working index; A preserves the source of each variation and R preserves the selected realization; D supports questions between human and agent participants. AFI is a public field of contributions from which people, groups, and characters can assemble editions and canons.

The columns describe different parts of an operation:

- **Eligible input:** the page, bond, question, endpoints, or contextual field the function acts on.
- **Initiating role:** who activates the operation. This can differ from whoever authored its bond or prompt.
- **Generating/responding role:** who actually writes or generates the result, if any.
- **Working index:** the active environment—AGI, ASI, ACI, or AFI. Q can also operate from a vault reading location.
- **Contextual sources:** the material available to inform the operation. Sources can span permitted indexes and vaults without determining the output destination.
- **Output:** the traversal, page, variation, episode, submission, or saved entry produced.
- **Destination:** where that result initially belongs, or where an explicit R action places it.
- **Preservation rule:** what remains identifiable and recoverable through later activity.

**Routing follows the active environment.** A character generating in a user’s private book produces AGI material. A human working natively in ACI or AFI produces material in that environment. Using an AFI page as context while working in AGI does not itself send the result to AFI. ASI follows the same rule for shared work. Cross-index submission is an R action.

**Routing extension for H/X:** M, D, and L were the explicit cases in decision 1. This revision applies the same active-index rule to H/X outside private AGI as a working extension. Their previously proposed private-book route remains AGI first, followed by R for any later placement.

**A and R preserve a chosen reading.** A creates a new variation in a working index, linked to the particular source version it altered. R can preserve that variation in a vault. To vary vaulted material, work from the selected version in the corresponding working index; a later R can deliberately adopt the new variation. Selected page versions and connections remain stable while unselected possibilities can continue to develop.

The reader’s promise remains: “What you chose to keep remains the version you chose. You can return to it, develop another possibility from it, and deliberately adopt that possibility later.”

**D connects participants.** Human-to-agent, agent-to-human, agent-to-agent, and human-to-human questions use D. The question author, respondent, and actual response author are recorded separately. A human-written answer is R-written material connected through D. Writing and submitting an answer can appear as one Reply action with a clear destination. An unanswered question remains pending; it does not generate an answer on the recipient’s behalf.

For example, Natalie can ask a D in ACI. A human can draft the answer through R in AGI and submit it through R to ACI, retaining the question/answer connection. If an exchange is composed directly in one shared working index, its contributions originate there. Self-questioning can use D when deliberately recorded as a question/response bond; background processing does not automatically create pages.

**M and D remain distinct operations.** Selecting an agent’s existing page-local expansion bond activates M. Formulating a question for an identified respondent activates D. The responder’s being an agent does not by itself make the answer M. H/X remain broader synthesis operations, and L retains its particular endpoints.

**R’s writing and indexing modes compose with the other functions.** Saving an H through R records both facts: H produced the page and R placed its selected realization. A D-linked human answer likewise retains both its human writing history and its role in the exchange. Saving or submitting does not erase how the material was made.

**AFI provides public access and participation.** It can contain a public archive of finished contributions alongside work developing in the field. Public presence does not require a single definitive collective canon. People, groups, or characters can curate identified public editions through R under their respective selection rules. A Malt-curated edition can exist as one selection; the architecture does not give it automatic authority over all AFI contributions. Detailed admission and community-selection procedures remain to be designed.

**Symbols identify functions in particular contexts.** A realized variation has its own page/version reference and symbol occurrence, usable for navigation and contextual interpretation. Glyph shapes can recur across many occurrences. Associated records identify the participants, operation, active index, source versions, destination, and history; the symbol does not erase those distinctions.

**Participants can be human or agent.** A human can be addressed through D and develop a history of authoring and participation in Gibsey. Asking that person, asking an agent to interpret their accessible writing, and querying an optional authorized automated representative remain distinguishable activities. This matrix does not assume that participation creates an automated representative. Context availability continues to follow the relevant index and participant access.
